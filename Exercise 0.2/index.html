Household energy · Australia

Know what your appliances really cost to run.

Electricity prices in Australia have climbed steadily over the past decade, and appliances that run quietly in the background, fridges, TVs, standby chargers, often add more to a power bill than people expect. This site walks through where that usage comes from and lets you estimate it for yourself.

Where household energy use comes from

Every appliance draws a certain wattage while running, and some continue to draw a small amount even when switched to standby. Multiplied across hours of use per day and days per year, small differences in wattage turn into noticeable differences on a bill. 02

Frequently asked questions

Cost depends on wattage, how many hours a day the appliance is used, and the price charged per kilowatt-hour by your electricity retailer. A high-wattage appliance used briefly can cost less than a low-wattage appliance left running all day.

A kilowatt-hour is the unit electricity retailers bill by. It represents one kilowatt of power used continuously for one hour, and is calculated by multiplying an appliance's wattage by the hours it runs, then dividing by 1000.

Individually, standby draw is small, but it adds up across every appliance left plugged in around a home, every day of the year. Switching devices off at the wall when not in use is a simple way to reduce it.

The calculator on the Televisions page is a demonstration tool built for this exercise. It gives a reasonable estimate based on the wattage, hours of use, and price you enter, but actual bills depend on your specific plan and tariff.
